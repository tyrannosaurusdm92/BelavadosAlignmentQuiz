Belavadös:  A  Unified  Summary  of  the  
World
 
and
 
Campaign
 
Belavadös  is  a  long-term  Dungeons  &  Dragons  5th  Edition  campaign  set  in  a  dark  steampunk  
fantasy
 
world
 
where
 
morality,
 
death,
 
divinity,
 
infrastructure,
 
and
 
social
 
order
 
are
 
all
 
built
 
on
 
systems
 
that
 
are
 
powerful,
 
compromised,
 
and
 
deeply
 
interconnected.
 
It
 
is
 
a
 
living
 
setting
 
rather
 
than
 
a
 
fixed
 
storyline.
 
The
 
world
 
continues
 
to
 
expand
 
through
 
new
 
regions,
 
factions,
 
discoveries,
 
and
 
player
 
decisions,
 
and
 
the
 
campaign
 
is
 
designed
 
to
 
support
 
long-running
 
continuity
 
while
 
remaining
 
flexible
 
enough
 
to
 
welcome
 
new
 
players
 
and
 
new
 
story
 
directions
 
over
 
time.
 
At  the  heart  of  Belavadös  is  a  central  idea:  nothing  in  the  world  is  simple.  Morality  is  not  reduced  
to
 
a
 
single
 
label.
 
Gods
 
are
 
not
 
distant
 
moral
 
guides
 
but
 
imprisoned
 
predators.
 
Death
 
is
 
not
 
a
 
passive
 
event
 
but
 
an
 
active
 
cosmic
 
force.
 
Civilization
 
is
 
powered
 
by
 
substances
 
and
 
institutions
 
derived
 
from
 
divine
 
captivity.
 
Every
 
race,
 
institution,
 
route
 
of
 
travel,
 
and
 
major
 
power
 
structure
 
reflects
 
that
 
history.
 
The
 
result
 
is
 
a
 
setting
 
where
 
politics,
 
religion,
 
industry,
 
magic,
 
mortality,
 
and
 
personal
 
ethics
 
constantly
 
shape
 
one
 
another.

The  tone  of  Belavadös  blends  atmospheric  fantasy,  horror,  industrial  decay,  mystery,  resilience,  
and

The

It

iron
 
rails,
 
rune-lit
 
engines,
 
fogbound

skyship
 
harbors,
 
bureaucratic
 
records,

sealed

containing
 
weakened
 
divine

This  is  a  setting  where  logistics  matter.  Travel  matters.  Administration  matters.  A  missing

Small

The

feels

because
 
everything
 
in

is

to
 
something

The  campaign  encourages  players  to  define  what  their  characters  protect,  pursue,  resist,  or  
attempt

change.
 
It
 
supports
 
tragedy,
 
investigation,
 
rebellion,
 
exploration,
 
and

while

leaving
 
room
 
for
 
collaborative
 
humor,
 
companionship,

moments
 
of
 
hope.
 
The  Moral  Framework  of  Belavadös  
Belavadös  rejects  the  traditional  nine-box  alignment  model  in  favor  of  a  four-axis  moral  profile.  
Instead
 
of
 
asking
 
whether
 
a
 
character
 
is
 
simply
 
good,
 
evil,
 
lawful,
 
chaotic,
 
or
 
neutral,
 
the
 
setting
 
treats
 
morality
 
as
 
a
 
blend
 
of
 
distinct
 
dimensions:
 
--- PAGE 2 ---
●  selfishness  versus  altruism  ●  chaoticness  versus  lawfulness  ●  individualism  versus  cooperation  ●  dishonor  versus  honor  
These  axes  are  independent.  A  person  can  be  lawful  but  selfish,  honorable  but  rebellious,  
cooperative
 
but
 
self-serving,
 
or
 
altruistic
 
while
 
distrusting
 
institutions.
 
That
 
separation
 
matters
 
because
 
it
 
lets
 
the
 
world
 
describe
 
people
 
more
 
precisely
 
and
 
more
 
honestly.
 
The  point  is  not  to  flatten  character  into  a  single  label.  It  is  to  understand  the  different  pressures  
that
 
shape
 
behavior.
 
Honor
 
is
 
not
 
the
 
same
 
as
 
obedience.
 
Cooperation
 
is
 
not
 
the
 
same
 
as
 
kindness.
 
Lawfulness
 
is
 
not
 
the
 
same
 
as
 
virtue.
 
Selfishness
 
is
 
not
 
automatically
 
cruelty.
 
Belavadös
 
recognizes
 
that
 
people
 
often
 
hold
 
mixed
 
motives
 
and
 
contradictory
 
loyalties,
 
and
 
the
 
setting
 
is
 
built
 
to
 
reflect
 
that
 
complexity.
 
The  alignment  system  also  uses  scores  rather  than  fixed  labels.  Characters  move  along  each  
axis
 
over
 
time
 
through
 
repeated
 
choices,
 
major
 
sacrifices,
 
betrayals,
 
and
 
long-term
 
patterns.
 
The
 
neutral
 
center
 
of
 
each
 
axis
 
is
 
a
 
true
 
center,
 
not
 
a
 
vague
 
placeholder.
 
A
 
single
 
kind
 
act
 
does
 
not
 
make
 
someone
 
altruistic,
 
and
 
one
 
cruel
 
decision
 
does
 
not
 
make
 
them
 
a
 
monster.
 
What
 
matters
 
is
 
the
 
pattern
 
that
 
emerges
 
over
 
time.
 
This  makes  the  world  more  realistic  and  more  playable.  It  allows  characters  to  be  morally  legible  
without
 
being
 
simplistic.
 
It
 
also
 
gives
 
NPCs
 
a
 
more
 
nuanced
 
way
 
to
 
respond.
 
Someone
 
may
 
admire
 
a
 
character’s
 
generosity
 
while
 
distrusting
 
their
 
lawlessness,
 
respect
 
their
 
honor
 
while
 
fearing
 
their
 
selfishness,
 
or
 
trust
 
their
 
cooperation
 
even
 
if
 
they
 
are
 
neither
 
innocent
 
nor

The
 
moral
 
system
 
is
 
not
 
just
 
a
 
game
 
mechanic.
 
It
 
is
 
part
 
of
 
the
 
setting’s
 
philosophy
 
about
 
what
 
people

The  Gods  Are  Not  Saviors  
Belavadös  is  ruled,  in  a  sense,  by  the  Gods.  But  these  are  not  benevolent  deities  or  moral

They

For  most  of  history,  the  Gods  ruled  openly.  They  walked  in  cities,  influenced  dynasties,  
controlled
 
magical

and
 
shaped
 
civilizations

Their

was
 
not
 
a
 
distant
 
religion.
 
It
 
was
 
a
 
system
 
of
 
extraction.
 
They
 
fed
 
on
 
mortals,
 
manipulated
 
institutions,
 
and
 
cultivated
 
dependency.
 
Worship
 
was

inseparable
 
from
 
addiction,
 
fear,
 
ecstasy,
 
and
 
political

Faith
