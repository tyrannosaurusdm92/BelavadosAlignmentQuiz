Earth ↔ Belavadös Time Conversion Guide
Player-facing dual-date standard for calendars, schedules, transit, journals, festivals, and lore documents
Primary rule: write the familiar Earth date/time first, then the Belavadös equivalent.
1. Purpose and Player-Facing Rule
This guide standardizes every time reference so players always receive a readable Earth date/time first, followed by the in-world Belavadös equivalent. The Belavadös calendar keeps its divine naming, compressed year, rest-day structure, and mythic drift logic, but table use remains easy because the Earth timestamp remains the anchor.
• For play notes, handouts, quest timers, travel schedules, and ATA transit boards, use Earth first.
• For lore, temples, prophecies, festivals, letters, and legal records, include Belavadös time immediately after Earth time.
• When a source only has a Belavadös date, add a parenthetical Earth equivalent as soon as it matters for player scheduling.
2. Core Calendar Model
Belavadös uses an 11-month civil year with 30 days per month, creating a clean 330-day civic cycle. The week remains seven days long but the names are divine, and the weekly rest/synchronization point moves to Ishtaday, the Wednesday-equivalent.
Guiding ratio: 1 Belavadös civil day = 0.9045 Earth days. Therefore, 1 Earth day = about 1.1056 Belavadös days.
Earth → Belavadös elapsed days: Belavadös days = Earth elapsed days ÷ 0.9045.
Belavadös → Earth elapsed days: Earth days = Belavadös elapsed days × 0.9045.

5. Fixed 30-Day Month Grid
For printed Belavadös calendars, every month repeats the same 30-day internal grid. This is best for temple schedules, monthly ritual cycles, almanacs, and civic posters. For live player scheduling, keep using the Earth weekday translation table.
6. Belavadös Clock System
Belavadös uses a familiar 24-hour display so players do not have to learn a new clock face. The labels are lore-facing: Belahours, Belaminutes, and Belaseconds. The day is shorter relative to Earth time, so the clock advances faster when mapped strictly.
Display abbreviation: Bh = Belahour, Bm = Belaminute, Bs = Belasecond. Example: 14:30 Bh = 14 Belahours, 30 Belaminutes.
7. Earth-to-Belavadös Conversion Procedure
Use this process whenever converting an Earth timestamp into a Belavadös companion timestamp.
• Start with the Earth date and time. Use local table time unless a campaign region requires another Earth timezone.
• Find the Earth day-of-year. For May 30 in a non-leap year, this is day 150.
• Convert Earth elapsed days into Belavadös elapsed days: Earth day-of-year ÷ 0.9045.
• Find the Belavadös cycle day inside the 330-day cycle. If the result exceeds 330, wrap into the next cycle.
• Month = every block of 30 days. Day of month = position inside that block.
• For player-facing weekday, translate the real Earth weekday into the Belavadös weekday name.
• Show the final result as Earth first, Belavadös second.
8. Drift Buffer / 35-Day Ghost
After Hathruna ends, the remaining Earth-year span can be treated as the Drift Buffer, 35-Day Ghost, Null Time, Uncounted Span, Temporal Surplus, or Compression Residue. This span keeps the civic calendar clean while giving the world a mythic explanation for the missing or unstable time.
• Ordinary month dates do not need to advance during this span.
• Seasonal correction, strange portal behavior, astronomical anomalies, or divine prison instability can occur here.
• For player ease, keep Earth dates active and mark the Belavadös side as Drift Buffer unless a specific lore event overrides it.
9. Ishta Sync Point
Ishtaday is the central weekly rest, synchronization, and portal-restriction day. Each Ishtaday at 12:00 Bh, the world can enter an Ishta Pulse or Ishta Sync Point.

11. Deity Naming Rules
All weekdays and months are named after gods. Single-deity names should preserve the god’s recognizable root. Merged months combine two deity identities and should carry a short theme for each side of the merge.
12. Quick Reference
• Earth first, Belavadös second.
• 1 Belavadös day = 0.9045 Earth days.
• 1 Earth day = about 1.1056 Belavadös days.
• 11 months × 30 days = 330 Belavadös civil days.
• The weekly rest day is Ishtaday, the Wednesday-equivalent.
• Use live Earth weekday mapping for player-facing schedules.
• Use fixed monthly grid for in-world almanacs and repeated ritual calendars.
• After Hathruna, mark the remaining Earth-year span as Drift Buffer / 35-Day Ghost unless a campaign event needs a specific treatment.
